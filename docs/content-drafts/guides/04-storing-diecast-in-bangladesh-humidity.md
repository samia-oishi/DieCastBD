Bangladesh is a hard country to be a carded collector in. Months of 80–90% humidity do
three things to a collection: cardboard absorbs moisture and **warps**, blister plastic
**clouds and yellows** faster than in dry climates, and exposed metal bases can spot with
**rust** in the worst monsoon weeks. None of it is dramatic day to day — you notice it a
year later, when the card that was mint isn't.

Here's what actually works, cheapest first.

## 1. Keep cards off exterior walls and away from windows

The two enemies are moisture and direct sunlight. Sunlight fades card art and yellows
blisters permanently — a west-facing shelf is the worst spot in the house. Interior walls,
away from the window line, cost nothing and remove most of the risk.

## 2. Put premium cards in protectors

A protector shell does two jobs in this climate: it stops physical damage (bent corners,
crushed bubbles) and it slows the humidity exchange around the card. For anything premium
— Car Culture upward — a ৳150–200 shell on a ৳1,500+ card is the best value insurance in
the hobby. Fold-over blister cases protect stored cards; PVC display cases let you hang or
stand a card without exposing it.

## 3. Use silica gel where the cars live

The little desiccant packets that come with shoes and electronics are exactly the right
tool. A few packets in a display cabinet or storage box, swapped or dried out every couple
of months in monsoon season, keeps the microclimate around the collection meaningfully
drier. Reusable colour-indicating silica (blue/orange beads) is available in Bangladesh
and can be recharged in an oven.

## 4. Closed display beats open shelves

An enclosed cabinet or acrylic case isn't just about dust — it damps out the daily
humidity swings that do the slow damage. If you're displaying loose cars, enclosed acrylic
with occasional silica gel is the standard collectors settle on.

## 5. For long-term boxed storage

Sealed plastic tubs, cards standing vertically (never stacked flat under weight), silica
inside, stored off the floor. Check twice a year. Don't tape anything to the cards
themselves, and never store cards leaning against each other's blisters.

## The one-line version

Out of sunlight, inside a protector, with silica gel nearby — do those three things and a
Bangladeshi collection survives the climate as well as one anywhere else.
